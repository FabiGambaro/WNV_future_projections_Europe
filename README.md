# Northward expansion of ecological suitability for human West Nile virus cases in Europe with increasing global warming

West Nile virus (WNV) is one of the most widespread mosquito-borne viruses in Europe, causing large outbreaks of human cases since 2018. Climate change is expected to have complex consequences for infectious diseases, and WNV is no exception to this. Investigating the associations between environmental (e.g., climatic, land-use) factors and disease distributions, and projecting these relationships in the short- and long-term, are crucial for raising awareness and informing strategic planning. Here, we estimated and projected ecological suitability for human WNV cases across Europe at current (i.e., 1.3°C) and future global warming levels (i.e., 1.9°C, 2.3°C, 2.5°C, and 2.8°C), selected based on the 2025 Emissions Gap Report projections for the end of the century under different policy scenarios. At the current warming level, ecologically suitable areas are mostly concentrated in southern and central Europe, across the Mediterranean basin and the Balkans. At increasing warming levels, suitability is projected to expand northward, increasing the number of people living in suitable areas. Ecological suitability was also the highest in socioeconomically vulnerable regions at all warming levels, a pattern with potentially important public health implications.

## Global warming levels 
Instead of fixed future periods, projections were made at global warming levels (GWLs): +1.3 °C (current) and +1.9, +2.3, +2.5 and +2.8 °C (from the UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2025). Climate data were sourced from the Inter-Sectoral Impact Model Intercomparison Project (ISIMIP3b), using 14 bias-adjusted global climate models (GCMs) from the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6) under three shared socioeconomic pathways (SSPs): SSP1-2.6 (sustainability), SSP3-7.0 (regional rivalry) and SSP5-8.5 (fossil-fuelled development). Land-use (LUH2) and human population (COMPASS) data, also used as predictors, were taken from the SSP3-7.0 scenario for all GWLs.

The time window of each GWL was defined as the 21-year period centred on the year in which the 21-year running mean of global surface air temperature first exceeds the level, relative to 1850–1900. For climate data, this was done separately for each GCM-SSP combination, using each GCM’s own baseline. For land-use and population data, it was done using the SSP3-7.0 marker scenario. Time windows are computed in scripts/warming_levels_timewindows.R

## Future projections
Projected ecological suitability for human WNV cases increases with warming. At the current warming level (+1.3 °C), suitable areas are concentrated in southern and south-eastern Europe. As warming increases to +2.8 °C, suitability expands northwards into central and northern Europe (Figure 2). As a result, the number of people living in ecologically suitable areas rises from about 202 million at +1.3 °C to about 300 million at +2.8 °C (median across GCM-SSP-BRT runs). Projections are computed in scripts/future_projections_GWL.R
